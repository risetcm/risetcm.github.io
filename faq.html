First Visit FAQ

New Patients

Frequently asked questions.

Straight answers to what I hear most — how treatment works, what to expect, and whether it's right for you. Don't see your question here? Just ask.

Getting Started

What should I expect at my first visit?

Your first visit is part conversation, part treatment. I'll walk through your health history, current concerns, and goals, followed by pulse and tongue diagnosis and your first treatment — usually 60–90 minutes altogether. Read more on our First Visit page.

Do I need a referral from my doctor?

No referral is required to book an appointment. If you're already working with a physician or specialist, I'm happy to coordinate care alongside them.

What conditions do you treat?

I see patients for women's health, fertility, pain, digestive health, stress and sleep, and healthy aging, among other concerns. If you're not sure whether I can help with something specific, just reach out — I'll let you know honestly.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on what you're treating and how long it's been building. Some people feel a shift after one or two visits; more established patterns typically call for a series of weekly sessions, tapering as symptoms improve. I'll talk through a realistic plan at your first visit.

During Treatment

Does acupuncture hurt?

Most people feel little more than a light pinch, if anything at all. The needles are hair-thin — nothing like an injection needle — and most patients find sessions deeply relaxing, often drifting off during the quiet rest time built into treatment.

What does a session actually feel like?

After the needles are placed, you'll rest quietly for 20–30 minutes while they work. Many people feel warm, heavy, or pleasantly drowsy; some fall asleep entirely. It's common to feel calm, or a little tired, afterward.

How long does a session take?

Your first visit runs 60–90 minutes. Follow-up visits are shorter, usually 45–60 minutes, and build on the plan we set together.

Will I need herbal medicine too, or just acupuncture?

It depends on your pattern. Many treatment plans combine acupuncture with herbal medicine for a fuller effect, but not every case calls for both — I'll recommend what actually fits your situation, not a fixed package.

Safety & Evidence

Is acupuncture safe?

Yes. I use sterile, single-use needles, and acupuncture is considered very low-risk in the hands of a licensed practitioner. I'll always ask about your health history and current medications before treating you.

Are the needles sterile and single-use?

Always. Every needle is sterile, individually packaged, and used once before being disposed of.

Is there research behind Chinese medicine?

Acupuncture in particular has a growing body of clinical research behind it, especially for pain, digestive issues, and stress-related conditions. I practice with both classical training and a modern clinical understanding, and I'm happy to talk through the evidence for your specific concern.

Can I use Chinese medicine alongside my regular doctor's care?

Yes — I see this as a complement to conventional care, not a replacement for it. I'll ask about your current providers, medications, and any treatment you're already receiving, and coordinate with your other providers when it's helpful.

Practical Details

Do you accept insurance?

Rise operates on a self-pay basis and doesn't bill insurance directly. [Add a note here if you'd like to mention superbills, HSA/FSA, or payment plans.] Reach out before booking if you have questions about cost.

What should I wear to my appointment?

Loose, comfortable clothing works best, since treatment points are often on the arms, legs, and torso. If you're coming in for facial rejuvenation, it helps to arrive with minimal makeup.

How often will I need to come in?

Most plans start with weekly visits, then space out as your symptoms improve. We reassess together at every visit rather than locking you into a fixed schedule.

Where are you located, and how do I book?

We're located by appointment in Manhattan Chinatown, NYC. The easiest way to book is through our Book Appointment link — or reach out first if you have questions.
